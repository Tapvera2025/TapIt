# Step 8 — Break Management (P2)

> **For agentic workers:** Implement task by task, with a review after each task. Use the repository's actual APIs and migrations as the source of truth when a sketch below differs. Do not treat a passing unit suite as the PostgreSQL gate.

**Goal:** Deliver §13 and PRD §9.5: dated break policies, deterministic breach evaluation, human review before attendance or pay changes, visible employee allowances, and the unresolved-breach gate needed by Step 9 payroll.

**Starting point (28 September):** Step 7 correction, auto-close and reconciliation code and migration `0062_corrections.sql` are present. Attendance already reads breaks through `readDay`, has `breaks_evaluated_version` on `attendance_record`, accepts a `breaksPaid` calculator input, and exposes `applyOverlay` / `removeOverlays` for breach consequences. No `break-management` module or `payroll_input` table exists. The web punch handler is still unregistered pending G1; Step 8 must expose the allowance API and make the warning computation callable by that handler without claiming the punch route is live.

**Architecture:** `break-management` owns policies, versions, assignments, rules and breaches. Attendance owns day calculations, effective events, overlays, the evaluation watermark and an independent break-evaluation revision; break-management reaches those through `AttendanceFacade`, never by querying its tables. A registered resolver port lets attendance obtain a dated, immutable break-policy snapshot while calculating, without importing a sibling module. A small payroll-owned writer port and an early `payroll_input` table let a confirmed `deduct-amount` become a named input in the same transaction; Step 9 owns the rest of payroll. The hourly evaluator selects closed, freshly calculated days by calculation version **or** break-evaluation revision, not by a recent-time window.

**Scope decisions to preserve:**

- No policy means no limit, no breach, and paid breaks (D19/Q2 proposal). Never seed a default policy or penalty number.
- A new policy version or assignment starts **after** the organization's current date. Earlier work dates keep their prior resolved policy (BM-16). History preview is read-only.
- Automated consequences stay in review mode initially. A policy can enable `autoApply` only after a named HR reviewer attests one complete prior month of resolved breach decisions; keep that evidence and audit it. `autoApply` defaults to false at every layer (BM-5/6).
- Occurrence eligibility is based on a qualifying violation of the relevant rule condition, before any decision about its consequence. A waived qualifying day still counts. Approved-leave and holiday violations remain visible as current `suppressed` findings and do not count. An advisory lower-limit finding does not count; an enforced lower-limit breach does. When leave or holiday suppression changes, invalidate the affected later week/month days and derive their occurrence numbers again.
- `deduct-amount` is included here. This plan moves only the `payroll_input` storage and a narrow writer into Step 8; salary structures, runs, payslips and publishing stay in Step 9. A published payslip cannot exist before Step 9; Step 9 must use the input-change event and frozen-input drift check to flag later waivers.
- BM-17 performance aggregates and a full analytics screen remain with the separate performance/reporting work. The breach queue and attendance-day provenance are in scope here.

## Task 2 — Pure resolver and evaluator

- [ ] Implement a pure policy resolution function over candidate assignments and versions. It uses the **day's stored placement and shift**, the work date and the fixed rank person → team → shift → position → department → organization; inside a rank, higher numeric priority wins and ascending assignment UUID breaks ties. It never uses today's department to judge a past day. An assignment is active only within its half-open effective range; a policy version is the latest with `effective_from <= workDate`.
- [ ] Implement a pure measurement function over `readDay`'s effective breaks: total minutes, longest single break and break count. `break-start`/`break-end` in the same second is zero; scan or departure ends an open break; overnight breaks use instants rather than local clock subtraction. For flexible/no-shift days, measure and enforce only total break time (BM-15).
- [ ] Apply grace in **minutes** to total and single upper limits and the lower total limit; count has no minute grace. Upper-limit violation requires `measured > upperLimit + graceMinutes` (60 + 5 with 63 minutes is clean). Lower-limit violation requires `measuredTotal < lowerLimit - graceMinutes`; with a 30-minute minimum and five-minute grace, 25 minutes is clean and 24 minutes violates. Lower limit is advisory by default. A required lower limit may make a breach, but a flexible day still cannot acquire a lateness-style consequence.
- [ ] Measure the violation first; if approved leave or holiday suppresses it, persist a current `suppressed` finding before rule selection or occurrence counting, with NULL matched rule/occurrence. Otherwise evaluate rules in ascending `ordinal` exactly once for the day; the first matching rule wins and no consequences stack. Day occurrence is one qualifying day, week is an ISO Monday–Sunday week, and month resets on the first **organization-local work date**. Count qualifying evidence days for the relevant condition with this fixed eligibility table; persist the matched rule and occurrence number for unsuppressed breaches:

  | Earlier day | Counts toward later occurrences? |
  |---|---|
  | Qualifying violation later waived by HR | Yes |
  | `suppressed` violation from approved leave | No |
  | `suppressed` violation from holiday | No |
  | Advisory lower-limit finding (`lower_enforced=false`) | No |
  | Required lower-limit breach (`lower_enforced=true`) | Yes, unless leave/holiday suppresses it |

- [ ] Pure tests: every assignment rank, higher-priority and ascending-UUID ties, dated versions, upper/lower grace boundaries (including 30/5/25 clean and 30/5/24 violation), lower advisory/required, each condition, ascending-ordinal first-match precedence, ISO-week/month boundaries, flexible exclusions, scan-ended and zero-length breaks, night shift, no-policy paid default, and every row of the occurrence eligibility table. Avoid tests that merely restate a constant.
